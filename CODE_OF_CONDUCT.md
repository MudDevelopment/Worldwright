## Purpose

Worldwright should support respectful collaboration among developers, designers, writers, testers, operators, moderators, and players participating in official project activities.

People must be able to ask beginner questions, identify problems, challenge technical decisions, and report misconduct without harassment or retaliation. Technical skill, sponsorship, organizational rank, and contribution history do not confer an exemption from these expectations.

Participation should be welcoming regardless of race, ethnicity, nationality, caste, religion, age, disability, body size, gender identity or expression, sex characteristics, sexual orientation, socioeconomic background, education, or level of experience. This list is not permission to mistreat people on grounds it does not name.

## Scope

Once adopted, this policy applies to project-controlled spaces and activities, including:

- Repositories, issues, pull requests, reviews, and project documentation.
- Official discussion forums, chat channels, meetings, and events.
- Official reference worlds, playtests, and project-operated services.
- Private messages connected to project participation or used to evade project restrictions.
- Public activity by a person acting as an authorized Worldwright representative.

Independent games built with Worldwright are not automatically governed or moderated by toolkit maintainers. Their operators must publish their own conduct, consent, privacy, reporting, and moderation policies. Official project spaces remain covered even when the topic concerns an independently operated game.

Off-platform conduct may be considered when it creates a direct, documented safety risk in project spaces or carries out project-related harassment. This is not a general mandate to police unrelated beliefs or private lives. Reviewers must record the connection to project safety and avoid ideological or personal retaliation.

## Expected Behavior

- Discuss ideas and evidence without demeaning the people presenting them.
- Explain requirements and criticism clearly; distinguish a blocker from a preference.
- Respect different communication styles and accessibility needs.
- Give credit and preserve authorship and source provenance.
- Respect another person's name, pronouns, boundaries, and choice not to engage.
- Keep conversation appropriate to the channel and its stated audience.
- Correct mistakes and misleading claims, including inaccurate test or security claims.
- Accept moderator directions while using the appeal process for disagreements.
- Help reduce conflict rather than drawing a crowd into a personal dispute.
- Obtain consent before recording meetings or redistributing private communications, subject to legitimate, limited reporting needs.

Constructive criticism, respectful disagreement, reporting defects, and good-faith complaints about governance are allowed. A code of conduct must not become a mechanism for silencing inconvenient technical findings or protecting favored maintainers from scrutiny.

## Unacceptable Behavior

Examples include:

- Harassment, threats, stalking, intimidation, or repeated unwanted contact.
- Slurs, dehumanizing statements, targeted humiliation, and discriminatory abuse.
- Unwanted sexual attention, sexualized harassment, or sexual material in professional collaboration spaces.
- Doxxing or disclosure of another person's sensitive personal information without authorization.
- Impersonation, falsified evidence, or deliberate misrepresentation of authority.
- Retaliation against a reporter, witness, reviewer, or person setting boundaries.
- Coordinated dogpiling, targeted disruption, or encouraging others to evade restrictions.
- Deliberately exposing credentials or private reports to punish someone.
- Using administrator, publisher, or reviewer access to pressure participants for unrelated favors.
- Persistent bad-faith obstruction, insults, or provocation after clear moderation direction.
- Knowingly fabricated accusations intended to harm another person.

An unsubstantiated report is **not** evidence of bad faith. A person may report sincerely and still be mistaken, lack complete evidence, or describe conduct that does not ultimately meet the policy threshold.

## Roleplay and Reference Worlds

Worldwright supports fictional worlds, but fiction is not an excuse to mistreat participants.

- Separate in-character conflict from out-of-character consent and safety.
- A character's fictional conduct does not automatically establish the player's personal beliefs; investigate context.
- A fictional role, faction rank, or “wizard” title does not confer real authority over another participant.
- Do not use emotes, character dialogue, scene mechanics, or private channels to continue harassment.
- Respect scene boundaries and requests to pause or disengage. No one must continue an unwanted scene to prove a complaint.
- Official reference content and playtests must publish audience and content expectations before inviting participants.
- Do not assume a software license authorizes use or publication of a player's private scene log.

Game rules may permit competitive conflict. They do not permit personal threats, coercion, nonconsensual disclosure, or retaliation.

## Reporting and Contact Status

| Function | Current status | Required before policy activation |
|---|---|---|
| Primary private reporting channel | Not established | Verified confidential route monitored by designated responders |
| Alternate channel for complaints involving a responder | Not established | Independently controlled route, not accessible to the accused responder |
| Appeal channel | Not established | Route to an uninvolved reviewer or agreed external reviewer |
| Response coverage and target times | Not established | Published hours, backup ownership, and achievable targets |
| Evidence storage and retention | Not established | Access controls, retention/deletion schedule, and incident-record policy |

When a channel is published, verify it through the official repository or project site. A random direct message claiming to be a moderator is not sufficient verification.

Until then:

1. Do not post sensitive evidence, accusations with private details, or victim identities in public issues.
2. If you already have an independently verified private contact for an uninvolved maintainer, request an appropriate reporting route.
3. Otherwise, make only a nonsensitive request for a private reporting channel, or use the hosting platform's established reporting process for conduct occurring there.
4. If a known responder is involved in the incident, avoid sending evidence through a channel they control.

This project is not an emergency response service. For immediate danger, use appropriate local emergency or platform safety resources.

## What a Report May Include

Share only what is necessary and safe:

- The relevant project space, date/time, and people involved.
- A factual account of what happened and why it concerns you.
- Links or limited excerpts, if available; full private logs are not automatically required.
- Whether the conduct is continuing or an immediate safety measure is needed.
- Any responder conflict of interest.
- A safe reply method and any confidentiality concerns.

Reports may come from affected people or witnesses. Anonymous reports should be accepted where the chosen channel permits them, while explaining that limited information can constrain investigation and follow-up.

You do not have to confront the reported person, negotiate with them, reveal unrelated personal history, or meet a legal burden of proof before seeking help.

## Fair Handling and Confidentiality

The adopted process should:

1. Acknowledge receipt within published, staffed response targets.
2. Check immediate risk and investigator conflicts.
3. Limit evidence access to people who need it for a defined role.
4. Preserve relevant evidence without unnecessary collection or redistribution.
5. Invite the reported person's response when safe and appropriate, without exposing a reporter unnecessarily.
6. Evaluate context, seriousness, repeated behavior, power imbalance, and evidence quality.
7. Choose a proportionate action and record the rationale.
8. Communicate an appropriate outcome and appeal route to the people concerned.
9. Monitor compliance and retaliation risk.

Do not promise absolute confidentiality. A fair investigation, safety obligations, or applicable law may require limited disclosure. Explain foreseeable limits and seek the reporter's preferences where feasible.

Private reports are not normal issue-tracker content. Do not place them in ordinary logs, public archives, training datasets, or unapproved AI tools. Retain them only under an approved schedule and legitimate case needs, including any applicable legal hold.

No finding should be based solely on popularity, seniority, or a reporter's membership in a favored group. Interim safety restrictions are not by themselves a final finding of wrongdoing.

## Conflicts of Interest

A responder must disclose and recuse for material conflicts, including being the subject of the report, a directly involved participant, or someone whose close personal or supervisory relationship reasonably compromises impartiality.

For significant sanctions, seek review by at least two uninvolved authorized people where staffing permits. A sole maintainer cannot honestly offer an independent appeal of their own decision; arrange an external or organizational reviewer before activating the policy.

If all available maintainers are conflicted, route the matter to the previously designated independent reviewer. Do not disclose the report to additional volunteers merely to assemble a panel.

## Possible Actions

Actions depend on impact and risk, not a mandatory sequence:

| Action | Typical purpose |
|---|---|
| Clarification or coaching | Address a misunderstanding or lower-impact first incident |
| Written warning | State the violation, required change, and consequences of repetition |
| Content removal or channel restriction | Stop harmful material or limit a specific source of disruption |
| Time-limited participation restriction | Protect participants and create space for review or behavior change |
| Removal of project privileges | Address misuse of review, moderation, publishing, or infrastructure access |
| Indefinite or permanent exclusion | Address severe harm, credible ongoing risk, or repeated serious violations |

Serious threats or exposure of private information may justify immediate containment without a prior warning. Record who acted, why, the scope, and when independent review will occur.

Where safe, explain restrictions, duration/review criteria, and permitted appeal contact. Do not require a public apology, forced reconciliation, or direct contact with the affected person as a condition of reporting or safety.

Restrictions on access to repositories, events, and services do **not**, by themselves, revoke rights already granted under an applicable software license. Legal license enforcement is a separate process.

## Appeals

Before adopting this policy, publish a private appeal route and an achievable submission/review window. Reasonable extensions should be available for accessibility or safety needs.

An appeal may identify:

- A material factual error or previously unavailable evidence.
- A conflict of interest or procedural failure.
- An action disproportionate to the documented conduct.
- Changed circumstances relevant to a time-limited restriction.

An uninvolved reviewer should assess it and provide a reasoned outcome. An appeal does not authorize continued prohibited contact or automatically suspend safety restrictions. No participant is required to retry the dispute publicly.

## Transparency and Policy Maintenance

Publish the adopted version, effective date, accountable roles, and reporting routes. Notify participants of material policy changes.

Where the community is large enough to preserve privacy, publish anonymized process statistics or lessons learned. Do not publish case details, reporter identities, or small-cell statistics that effectively identify participants.

Policy adoption requires:

- Owner approval and a named accountable community lead.
- Verified primary, independent alternate, and appeal channels.
- Staffed coverage and realistic response targets.
- Investigator training and conflict-of-interest procedures.
- Evidence access and retention rules.
- A clear relationship to hosting-platform policies and independent game operators.
